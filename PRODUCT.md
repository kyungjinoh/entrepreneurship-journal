# Product

## Register

brand

## Platform

web

## Users

General readers curious about how things get built — people who enjoy following a real builder's journey. The site should feel like visiting someone's personal corner of the web, not reading a publication's front page. Secondary audience: people evaluating the author's work (collaborators, employers, fellow builders) who want a quick scan of projects and writing.

## Product Purpose

A personal website and living archive: a project-by-project log of entrepreneurship and engineering, where each project *is* a journal entry documenting how it was built. The homepage opens with the author's headshot and a short intro, then leads with featured build-logs and the rest of the projects below. Every entry carries one image so the archive reads visually, not as a wall of text. Visitors can browse builds, read the story behind each one, and understand what the author is working on now. Success looks like someone landing on the site, grasping who this person is within seconds, and opening at least one featured build-log.

## Positioning

Intimate access to a builder's notebook — entrepreneurship and engineering, told project by project, with the warmth of a personal site rather than the distance of a media brand.

## Conversion & proof

- **Primary CTA:** Read what is featured first — the author controls pinned/featured ordering so the most important work surfaces at the top.
- **Secondary CTA:** None strong; if the featured entry lands, that is enough. Archive browsing is available but not pushed.
- **The line a visitor remembers after 10 seconds:** One builder's log — entrepreneurship and engineering, project by project.
- **Belief ladder:**
  1. This is a real person building real things, not a content farm.
  2. The writing is specific and honest — worth a few minutes of attention.
  3. There is a thread here (projects, not isolated posts).
  4. Open the featured entry or a project that catches the eye.
- **Proof on hand:** The author's own project history and journal entries. No external testimonials or press logos at launch; credibility comes from specificity and the work shown.

## Brand Personality

Personal, documentary, maker-forward — and visually alive. Casual and direct like a personal site you'd bookmark, but confident enough to use bold image cards, a scrolling name marquee, and purposeful motion rather than staying flat and quiet. Primary references: [thijs.gg](https://thijs.gg/) (headshot intro, animated name marquee, "Now / Previously" bio, image-led project grid with year tags and star-featured cards) and [derrickbassey.com](https://www.derrickbassey.com/) (headshot, evolving project record, structured work sections with clear metadata). Secondary sensibility from personal Substacks and Are.na (individual voice, curated visual gallery). Voice is first-person, specific, and unhurried; the presentation is energetic but never hype-driven.

## Anti-references

- SaaS landing page clichés — hero metrics, gradient accents, eyebrow kickers on every section. (Image cards are welcome here; *identical, soulless* card grids are not — each build-log should feel like its own object, the way thijs.gg's projects do.)
- Corporate blog — sanitized PR voice, stock imagery in place of the author's real project photos, "thought leadership" tone.
- Dark-mode AI tool aesthetic — purple gradients, glass cards, glow effects, hustle-energy typography.
- Hustle culture performance — motivational framing, bro-energy, vanity metrics as design.

## Design Principles

1. **Personal site, not publication.** The visitor should feel they met a person, not subscribed to a brand. It opens with a headshot and intro; projects and writing live together on one cohesive surface.
2. **Projects are the spine, images are the hook.** Each build-log is a journal entry about real work in progress, and each one carries one image so the archive is scanned visually before it's read.
3. **Editorial control without complexity.** Featured/pinned ordering (star-marked, like thijs.gg) lets the author decide what matters now; the homepage reflects current priorities, not just recency.
4. **Alive, not loud.** Bold image cards and purposeful motion (a name marquee, tasteful hover and entrance animation, subtle moving patterns) give the site energy — without tipping into gradient/glow hype.
5. **Show the work, don't pitch it.** Let real project photos, dates, and specifics do the talking. No hero-metric templates or conversion theater.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.1 AA baseline: sufficient contrast on body text, semantic HTML, keyboard-navigable interactive elements, and `@media (prefers-reduced-motion: reduce)` respected for any motion. Personal sites often under-invest here; this one should not.
